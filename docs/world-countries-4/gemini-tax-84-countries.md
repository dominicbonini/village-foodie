To determine where HatchGrab Ltd can sell its B2B SaaS subscriptions without registering for foreign taxes, the core legal trap to understand is how indirect tax laws define **"B2B"** versus **"B2C"**.

In almost every jurisdiction with a digital services VAT/GST regime (including the EU, EEA, Eastern Europe, and recent OECD-modeled regimes across Africa and Asia), **an unregistered small business or sole trader is legally classified as a private consumer (B2C)** unless they hold and provide a valid local VAT/GST registration number. Because your target customers are small food trucks that are mostly *not* VAT/GST-registered, selling to an unregistered food truck triggers non-resident "B2C" digital service registration rules in those countries.

### Special Questions

#### A. United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Oman (B2B Reverse Charge & Unregistered Customers)

* **If you ONLY accept VAT-registered businesses (and verify their TRN/VAT ID at signup):** **You do NOT need to register** in the UAE (5% VAT), Bahrain (10% VAT), or Oman (5% VAT). In all three GCC states, when a non-resident supplier with no local establishment supplies electronic services exclusively to VAT-registered businesses, the customer must self-account for the VAT under the **reverse charge mechanism**, relieving the foreign supplier of any obligation to register.
* **If a customer is NOT VAT-registered:** **Yes, you must register from the very first sale**. There is **zero registration threshold** (`Nil`) for non-resident suppliers in the UAE, Bahrain, and Oman (the domestic thresholds—such as AED 375,000 in the UAE—apply strictly to resident businesses). Any supply to an unregistered small food truck is treated as a B2C supply where the non-resident supplier must register and charge VAT.
* **UAE Free Zones / Designated Zones:** UAE "Designated Zones" are treated as outside the UAE **only for supplies of physical goods** (subject to strict customs controls). Under Article 51 of the UAE VAT Executive Regulations, **the place of supply for services (including electronic/SaaS services) inside a Designated Zone or Free Zone is still treated as onshore mainland UAE**. Therefore, selling SaaS to an unregistered business located in a Dubai/UAE Free Zone still triggers mandatory UAE VAT registration from the first dirham.
* **Sources:** [UAE FTA E-Commerce VAT Guide VATGEC1](https://tax.gov.ae/), [Oman Tax Authority E-Commerce Guide](https://tms.taxoman.gov.om/), [Bahrain NBR Digital Economy Guide](https://www.nbr.gov.bh/), [Lookuptax GCC Overview (Sep 2026)](https://www.lookuptax.com/docs/explainers/vat-on-digital-services-non-resident-suppliers).

#### B. Canada — Provincial Sales Taxes (BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba) for B2B SaaS

Unlike federal GST/HST and Quebec QST (which exempt non-residents from charging tax when the buyer provides a valid GST/HST or QST registration number), **British Columbia, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba operate traditional retail Provincial Sales Taxes (PST/RST)**. Under PST/RST rules, **businesses are considered the "end user" of SaaS** unless they are purchasing the software strictly for *resale* to their own customers. Because a food truck uses HatchGrab to run its kitchen and take food orders (rather than reselling software licenses), **B2B SaaS is fully taxable** and there is no B2B reverse-charge exemption that removes the foreign seller's registration duty once the provincial threshold is crossed:

1. **Saskatchewan (6% PST):** **Requires registration from the first dollar ($0 CAD threshold)**. Non-resident vendors selling SaaS ("electronic distribution services" / computer services) to any end-user in Saskatchewan—including businesses—must register for and collect 6% SK PST with no small-supplier threshold. *(You cannot sell to Saskatchewan customers without registering).*
2. **British Columbia (7% PST):** **Requires registration only above CAD 10,000 per year** in BC sales. Non-resident sellers located outside Canada who sell software/SaaS for use on devices in BC must register only if their gross revenue from BC sales of software and telecom services exceeds **CAD 10,000** in the preceding 12 months (or is reasonably estimated to exceed CAD 10,000 in the next 12 months). Below CAD 10,000/yr in BC, you do not need to register.
3. **Manitoba (7% RST):** **Requires registration only above CAD 30,000 per year**. Out-of-province vendors selling taxable software/SaaS to Manitoba purchasers (including business end-users) are exempt from registering until their total taxable sales starts exceeding **CAD 30,000** per year.
4. **All Other Provinces:** Alberta, Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut have no provincial sales tax (0%). Ontario, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI, and Newfoundland & Labrador use HST (covered by the federal GST/HST B2B exemption if the customer gives a valid GST/HST number, with a CAD 30,000 threshold for unregistered customers).

* **Sources:** [Government of BC Bulletin PST 105 (Software)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/taxes/sales-taxes/pst), [Saskatchewan Information Bulletin PST-74](https://sets.saskatchewan.ca/), [PwC Canada Corporate Other Taxes (June 2026)](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/canada/corporate/other-taxes), [PayProGlobal SK SaaS Guide (2026)](https://payproglobal.com/saas-sales-tax/saskatchewan/).
