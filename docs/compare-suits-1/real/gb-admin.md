## In short

If your customers should order ahead on their phone and choose a collection time, you need an ordering system, not just a till. Look for four things: collection times that follow your kitchen capacity; a weekly schedule for changing locations; low or no fees per order; and no long contract. Card readers and tills cover payment at the truck and complement an ordering system.

## What the features mean

- **Pre-orders with a chosen collection time:** Customers order in advance and choose the time they collect.
- **Collection times limited by kitchen capacity:** Customers only see collection times the kitchen can actually make.
- **Order ahead for a later day or event:** Customers can order today for a market or event on a later day.
- **Weekly schedule with changing locations:** The truck's week of places and times; customers order for the place and day it is there.
- **Walk-up orders on the same screen as online orders:** Orders taken at the window go into the same list as online orders.
- **Sold-out and stock limits per event:** Mark dishes sold out and set how many of each can be sold at an event.
- **Customers told when their order is ready:** How customers hear their order is ready: SMS, WhatsApp, email, push or a live order-status page.
- **Allergens on the menu:** Each dish shows its allergens to customers before they order.
- **Menu set up from a photo:** A photo or PDF of the menu fills in the dishes for you to check.
- **Online card payments incl. Apple Pay and Google Pay:** Customers pay online by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay when they order.
- **Kitchen display (KDS):** Orders appear on a screen in the kitchen, in the order to make them.
- **Printed kitchen tickets:** Each order prints on a receipt printer in the truck.
- **Keeps working if the internet drops:** Orders and changes made without a connection are kept and sent once it is back.
- **Several vans or trucks:** One account runs more than one truck.
- **Own web address:** The ordering page on the business's own domain.
- **WhatsApp auto-replies:** Customers' WhatsApp messages are answered automatically.
- **Social media posts from your schedule:** Ready-made posts showing where the truck is this week.
- **Listed on a food-truck discovery map:** Customers find the truck on a public map of food trucks near them.
- **iPad app:** The truck's app for taking and managing orders on an iPad.
- **Android app:** The same app on an Android tablet or phone.
- **Hardware supplied (tablet, printer):** The vendor provides the tablet or the printer.
- **Card reader for the window supplied:** The vendor provides a card reader for payments at the truck.
- **Delivery:** Orders delivered to the customer's address.
- **Monthly price:** What the business pays each month for the software.
- **Fee per online order:** What the vendor charges on each online order, on top of the monthly price.
- **Card fee:** The payment provider's fee on each card payment.
- **Minimum contract:** How long the business is tied in before it can cancel.
- **Set-up fee:** A one-off charge to start.
- **Free trial:** Time to use the system for free before paying.

## Which system suits whom?

**HatchGrab suits you if …** you trade at changing locations, want pre-orders with a collection time, don't want your kitchen overloaded, and don't want a contract.

**HatchGrab doesn't suit you if …** your customers should pay with PayPal or Klarna, or you want a certified till with a TSE from one supplier.

**Tills and card machines:** Tills take payment at the truck and cover the TSE requirement. An ordering system works alongside a till; it does not replace it.

**Delivery platforms:** Delivery platforms bring new customers but take commission on every order. Uber Eats states 14% for collection and 30% for delivery by Uber couriers in Germany. Wolt says on its own website that it charges commission but gives no rates. We could not check Lieferando’s rates. That commission applies to regulars who pre-order with you, too.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a food truck ordering system?

An ordering system takes orders online before customers reach the truck: they choose dishes and a collection time, pay online or on collection, and the order appears in your kitchen. A till or card machine takes payment at the truck. The two complement each other.

### What does HatchGrab cost in United Kingdom?

Pay as you go: £0 a month, 0.99% per online card order; Pro: £29 a month, online orders up to £4,000 a month included, then 0.99%; Unlimited: £69 a month, no HatchGrab fee on online orders. Online card payments also carry Stripe’s fees: 1.5% + 20p per card payment. The first 3 months are free, with every feature.

### Is there a minimum term?

No. There is no minimum term and no notice period. You can cancel any time; the fee is billed monthly in advance, and you keep access until the end of the month you have paid for.

### Does it work with changing locations?

Yes. You set up your weekly schedule with places and times, and customers order for the place and the day you are there.

### How does the system stop my kitchen being overloaded?

Customers only see collection times your kitchen can actually make. The system works from each dish's preparation time and how many you prepare at once.

### Do I still need a till with a TSE?

HatchGrab is not a till and has no TSE. How you record online orders in your till is a question for your tax adviser.

### Which payment methods does HatchGrab support?

Customers pay online by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, or pay at the truck. PayPal and Klarna are not supported at the moment.
